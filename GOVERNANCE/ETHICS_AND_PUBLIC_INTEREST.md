# Ethics and public-interest guardrails

## Public-interest objective

Reduce uncertainty, incomplete submissions, duplicate-payment risk, and opaque decisions without weakening due process or shifting legal judgment to software.

## Guardrails

- The service supports both payment/acceptance and contest; it does not pressure either choice.
- It does not sell leads, promote loans, take a percentage of penalties, or prioritize cases by ability to pay.
- It does not score citizen credibility or predict guilt.
- It does not conceal contrary evidence from the citizen or reviewer.
- It does not create fabricated documents, statements, or grounds.
- It does not use dark patterns around deadlines or payment.
- It offers an assisted and non-AI path.
- Review quality and SLA metrics must not incentivize careless mass rejection.
- Any future authority deployment needs a clear appeal, correction, and human-accountability mechanism.

## Fairness questions before a feature ships

- Who cannot use this because of device, language, disability, identity, or connectivity?
- Could this feature turn a data error into a legal disadvantage?
- Can a person see and correct the facts used about them?
- Is the next-action owner explicit?
- Does the feature change a right, deadline, money, or access to services?
- If AI is wrong or unavailable, can the person still complete the task?
- Does the metric reward speed at the expense of a reasoned review?

## Winning responsibly

The product should compete through clarity, completion, inclusion, safety, and operational credibility. It should not copy private work, exploit real citizens, bypass government controls, or exaggerate integrations. A truthful, reliable live system is a stronger entry than a broader fictional one.
