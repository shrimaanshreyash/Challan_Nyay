# Design principles

## 1. Resolution before decoration

The primary screen should answer the current case state, deadline, owner, and next action. Visual polish supports comprehension; it does not replace it.

## 2. One service, many jurisdictions

Keep the citizen mental model stable while showing jurisdiction-specific rules, authority, coverage, and destination. Never hide differences behind a false national-uniformity claim.

## 3. Explain before asking

Before a number, CAPTCHA, identity check, document upload, or declaration, explain why it is required, how it will be used, and what happens next.

## 4. Route before collecting

Identify case type and jurisdiction early. Do not collect a long submission only to reveal that the product cannot route it.

## 5. Deadlines are product objects

Every displayed date includes:

- the event that started the clock;
- the rule or jurisdiction source;
- calendar days remaining;
- the consequence of action or inaction;
- uncertainty or paused-clock conditions where applicable.

## 6. Evidence, not essays

Prefer structured allegation facts, a selected ground, ground-specific questions, and an evidence checklist over a large free-text complaint box. Free text supplements, never replaces, the structured packet.

## 7. No black-box status

Show who owns the next action, what last changed, expected timing, why a request was made, and the possible next states.

## 8. AI stays visible and correctable

Label extracted or suggested content. Show source snippets or image regions where useful. Require confirmation. Never auto-submit or make a decision.

## 9. Trust without impersonation

Use calm civic design, source links, timestamps, receipts, signed-order metadata, and disclaimers. Do not borrow seals, national emblems, official mastheads, or confusing domains.

## 10. Design for interrupted lives

Autosave locally and server-side when authenticated. Support resumable uploads, clear recovery, low-bandwidth mode, and assisted service. Never erase a draft because a session expired.

## 11. Language is functionality

Short sentences, active voice, familiar verbs, localized examples, correct numerals/dates, and reviewed translations are core product behavior.

## 12. Accessibility is an acceptance criterion

Keyboard, focus, screen-reader semantics, zoom, contrast, touch targets, accessible authentication, and error recovery are tested—not assumed from a component library.
